# Code of conduct

This project is a technical standards community. Participation requires rigorous disagreement
without personal degradation.

## Expected conduct

Contributors are expected to:

- discuss claims, evidence, risks, and tradeoffs rather than speculate about a person's
  motives or competence;
- make review feedback specific enough to act on, including a path, rule ID, failure mode, or
  reproducible observation where available;
- distinguish normative objections from style preferences;
- disclose conflicts of interest that could materially affect doctrine or dependency choices;
- protect private vulnerability reports and personal information;
- credit sources and prior contributions;
- accept that a guarantee may be narrowed when its evidence is insufficient;
- and allow maintainers to pause a discussion that has become hostile or repetitive.

Examples of unacceptable conduct include harassment, threats, discriminatory language,
sexualized attention, doxxing, deliberate disclosure of secrets, sustained disruption,
fabricated evidence, impersonation, and retaliation against good-faith reporting.

Strong technical criticism is permitted. A statement such as "this constructor allows the
documented invariant to be bypassed" is appropriate when supported by code. Attacking the
author is not.

## Scope and enforcement

The policy applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, release
coordination, and other project spaces. It also applies when someone represents the project
in a public venue.

Report conduct concerns privately to repository maintainers through GitHub's repository
contact mechanisms. Include relevant links, dates, and context. Do not publish sensitive
details merely to prove that a report exists.

Maintainers may remove content, request correction, issue a private warning, restrict
participation, impose a temporary suspension, or permanently exclude a participant. The
response should be proportionate to impact, pattern, intent, repair, and risk to others.
Maintainers with a conflict must recuse themselves. Retaliation or repeated evasion increases
severity.

Good-faith mistakes should receive a clear correction path. Deliberate abuse, credible
threats, or exposure of private data may require immediate restriction. Enforcement records
should preserve enough evidence for consistency while minimizing retained personal data.

This policy is informed by the [Contributor Covenant
2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/) and adapted to a
doctrine and evidence-review project.
